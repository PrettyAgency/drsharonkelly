Grommets

Also called middle ear ventilation tubes, or simply tubes.

Overview

Grommets are small tubes inserted through the eardrum to allow ventilation of the middle ear — the airspace behind the eardrum. For the ear to work normally, this space needs to contain air at normal atmospheric pressure. There are different designs of grommets; most commonly short- to medium-stay designs are used, though on occasion long-stay grommets may be required.

Short- to medium-stay grommets last on average 9 to 12 months, though they may extrude earlier or last up to 2 years. On rare occasions they do not come out by themselves and may need to be removed. Long-stay grommets are designed to remain in place for several years.

What Happens

In Children

A simple procedure performed as day surgery. General anaesthesia is administered via a gaseous technique — no breathing tubes or needles are required in most cases. Using a microscope, a small incision is placed in the eardrum and the grommet is positioned through it.

In Adults

In most cases this can be performed under topical anaesthesia in the consulting room, without the need for hospitalisation and no injections required. Occasionally, narrow or difficult ear canals may require day surgery under general anaesthesia. Return to normal activities is usually immediate.
